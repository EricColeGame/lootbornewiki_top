Few moments in Lootborne hit harder than the glow of a sealed container sitting just out of reach. A Lootborne loot cache is the game's reward loop in miniature: find it, earn the right to open it, and walk away with gear that actually changes how your character plays. Every Lootborne loot cache type — from humble supply crates to vault-grade containers — follows slightly different rules, and knowing those rules is the difference between a stash full of filler and a build that keeps pace with the content.

This guide breaks down what each cache family is worth, how rarity and loot tables interact, where to farm efficiently, and the small habits that stop you from wasting the best containers you find.

## What Counts as a Lootborne Loot Cache?

In Lootborne, "cache" is a broad label. It covers anything you have to claim rather than simply pick up off the floor: crates behind a locked door, containers that drop after a boss, reward chests at the end of a timed run. They share one trait — they gate their contents behind a condition. Sometimes that condition is a key, sometimes it is a fight, and sometimes it is simply surviving long enough to reach them.

Because of that, caches are not interchangeable. A supply crate you pass on the road and a vault container at the end of a high-difficulty run may both say "cache," but they pull from completely different reward pools. Treating them the same is the fastest way to burn time on containers that were never going to give you anything meaningful.

| Cache Family | Typical Location | Usual Rarity Band | Respawn Behavior | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supply cache | Open world, camps, ruins | Common to Uncommon | Refreshes when the area resets | Materials, currency, early gear |
| Locked cache | Guarded rooms, side vaults | Rare | Needs a key or puzzle solve | Filling specific gear slots |
| Boss cache | End of dungeons and arenas | Rare to Epic | Once per clear | Build-defining drops |
| Vault cache | Timed events, high difficulty | Epic to Legendary | Locked on a weekly-style timer | Endgame chase items |
| Event cache | Seasonal or limited-time modes | Variable | Only during the event window | Cosmetics, exclusive affixes |

If you take one thing from this table, make it this: the lower rows are worth routing your entire session around, and the top rows are worth grabbing only when they happen to be on your path.

## Rarity Tiers and How Cache Loot Tables Actually Work

Every Lootborne loot cache rolls from a weighted loot table. Higher tiers are not simply "better" — they are narrower. A common cache pulls from a wide pool of materials, currency, and low-tier gear, which makes it reliable but rarely exciting. A legendary-band cache pulls from a much smaller pool, which is why it can hand you an item that defines your build for the next several hours of play.

| Rarity Band | Drop Frequency | Where It Shows Up | What To Do With Duplicates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Common | Very frequent | Supply caches, trash clears | Salvage for materials |
| Uncommon | Frequent | Supply and locked caches | Salvage or sell |
| Rare | Occasional | Locked and boss caches | Keep one, salvage the rest |
| Epic | Uncommon | Boss and vault caches | Stash for alt builds |
| Legendary | Rare | Vault and event caches | Never salvage without checking affixes |

Player experience suggests that the gap between Epic and Legendary is where most frustration lives. The drop pool is small, the competition for the roll is high, and a single bad roll can feel like a wasted run. Community reports point to the same coping strategy across most loot-driven games: run the content that produces the most rolls per hour rather than chasing a single perfect container.

Many builds of the game also include some form of bad-luck protection that nudges your odds upward after a dry streak. Whether that applies to the Lootborne loot cache you are opening right now depends on your patch, so it is worth checking the official notes rather than assuming.

## Where to Find Lootborne Loot Caches

Cache density matters more than cache quality for most players, because density is something you control. You can choose a route. You cannot choose a roll. The zones below are grouped by how they reward different playstyles.

| Zone Type | Cache Density | Risk Level | Time Per Cache | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## How to Open a Lootborne Loot Cache the Right Way

Opening a cache looks like a single button press. In practice, the decisions around it are what separate efficient players from everyone else.

| Step | Action | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear all nearby enemies first | Some caches can be interrupted or despawned mid-fight |
| 2 | Check for keys, levers, or objectives | A locked cache you cannot open is a wasted trip |
| 3 | Pop buffs and consumables before opening | Loot-affecting bonuses usually apply at the moment of the roll |
| 4 | Open in a group when the cache is instanced per player | Everyone gets their own roll instead of splitting one |
| 5 | Sort, lock, and stash immediately | Prevents accidental salvage on the next run |

Steps three and five are the ones players skip most often. Consumables that influence loot are usually wasted if you pop them after the container is already open, and the number of build-defining items lost to a careless salvage click is genuinely tragic.

### Solo vs. Group Opening

- **Solo:** Full control over pacing, no coordination overhead, and every cache on your route is yours.
- **Group:** Better for boss and vault caches where the container is instanced per player, and safer in high-difficulty zones where solo clearing is slow.
- **Mixed approach:** Farm open-world caches solo for speed, then group up specifically for the high-tier containers at the end of a run.

## Farming Tips, Mistakes, and Efficiency

Good cache farming is mostly about removing friction. The players who pull ahead are not luckier — they simply open more containers per hour with fewer wasted steps.

**Do these:**

- Set a route and repeat it until it becomes muscle memory, so you stop thinking about navigation.
- Prioritize zones where caches respawn on an area reset rather than a long timer.
- Track which cache families actually feed your build, and skip the rest without guilt.
- Bank keys and unlock items instead of spending them the moment you find one.
- Keep a dedicated stash tab for "maybe later" gear so your inventory never blocks a run.

**Avoid these:**

- Opening a cache mid-fight and losing the contents to an interruption.
- Hoarding every Rare drop until your stash is unusable.
- Farming a single boss cache for hours when a faster route gives more total rolls.
- Ignoring event caches because they look inconvenient — the exclusive affixes rarely come back.
- Assuming a higher difficulty always means a better Lootborne loot cache. It usually means a better roll pool and a much higher failure rate.

| Priority | Cache Type | When To Farm It | Expected Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | Vault and event caches | Whenever the window is open | Chase items, exclusive affixes |
| High | Boss caches | When you need a specific slot | Targeted upgrades |
| Medium | Locked caches | When you already hold the key | Reliable Rare-tier gear |
| Low | Supply caches | Passive, on any route | Materials and currency |

The single biggest efficiency gain for most players is simply not stopping. A cache you open while already moving through a zone costs you almost nothing; a cache you detour twenty minutes for had better be a vault container.

## FAQ

**What exactly is a Lootborne loot cache?**
It is any sealed container in Lootborne that requires a condition to claim — a key, a cleared room, a completed objective, or a finished boss fight. The contents roll from a weighted loot table tied to that cache's rarity band.

**Do caches respawn, and how often?**
It depends on the family. Open-world supply caches generally refresh when the area resets, boss caches typically reset once per clear, and vault-style caches usually sit behind a longer weekly-style lockout. Check the current patch notes for exact timers.

**Is it worth pushing to higher difficulty for better cache loot?**
Usually yes for vault and boss caches, where the roll pool improves noticeably. For common supply caches, the upgrade is small enough that a faster, easier route often produces more total value per hour.

**Should I open a Lootborne loot cache immediately or save it?**
Open it when your loot-affecting buffs are active and your inventory has room. There is rarely a benefit to hoarding an unopened cache, but there is a real cost to opening one while you are mid-fight or nearly full on stash space.
