## Reading Loot: Rarity, Rolls, and What to Keep

Most loot games — Lootborne included — use a color-coded rarity ladder, and the trap is assuming the brightest border always wins. It doesn't. A perfectly rolled mid-tier item with the right affixes will outperform a badly rolled top-tier item in almost every build, and a lootborne guide that ignores this is setting you up for a wasted stash.

| Tier (generic) | Visual cue | Typical role | Keep or scrap? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base | Plain border | Leveling filler | Scrap unless it fills an empty slot |
| Mid | Colored border | Early build filler | Keep one or two, scrap the rest |
| High | Bright border, extra affix lines | Core build pieces | Keep when affixes match your plan |
| Top | Distinct animation or unique effect | Build-defining | Almost always keep — read it before scrapping |

Names and colors change between updates, so always confirm in the tooltip before you commit materials.

When you're staring at two similar drops, ask these questions in order:

- **Affix synergy:** Do the extra stats feed the skill or weapon you're actually using?
- **Roll quality:** Are the numbers near the top of their range, or scraping the bottom?
- **Upgrade room:** Can this piece be rerolled, socketed, or enhanced further?
- **Slot replacement:** Is it beating your current item, or just looking shinier?

Answer those four honestly and you'll vendor far less gear you later regret.

## Build Planning Without Wasting Your Best Drops

Most lootborne guide advice focuses on what to farm, but the harder skill is deciding what to do with a great drop once it lands. Scattered gearing is the single most common reason players stall out in mid-game content: five decent items that don't talk to each other lose to three mediocre items that do.

Work through this priority order before spending any upgrade materials:

| Priority | Question to ask | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Core identity | What one effect am I building around? | Prevents scattergun gearing |
| 2. Damage or utility engine | Which skill or weapon carries the build? | Focuses every affix decision |
| 3. Defensive floor | Can I survive two mistakes in a row? | Damage means nothing if you're dead |
| 4. Cheap gap fillers | What slots can I ignore for now? | Saves materials for real upgrades |
| 5. Optimization | Where are my two weakest pieces? | Last step, never the first |

A simple habit helps: before each session, write down the one item you're hunting. If a drop doesn't serve that goal or clearly beat what you have, it goes to the stash or the vendor. Player experience consistently shows that focused farming beats broad farming over a week of play.

## Progression Tips and Common Mistakes

Community reports and player experience point to the same handful of missteps over and over. None of them are dramatic on their own, but together they can add hours of unnecessary grinding.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chasing rarity over synergy | High-tier gear with wrong affixes underperforms | Compare affix lines, not border colors |
| Hoarding everything | Stash clutter slows every decision | Keep, sell, or scrap within seconds |
| Skipping crafting systems | Reroll and upgrade tools go unused | Learn one system per play session |
| Over-farming easy content | Rewards plateau quickly | Push difficulty when clears feel comfortable |
| Ignoring group etiquette | Friction with random teammates wastes runs | State your goal before the run starts |

Three habits worth building early:

1. **Set a session goal.** One boss, one upgrade, or one difficulty step — not "farm stuff."
2. **Rotate content.** Alternating zones keeps drop pools varied and prevents burnout.
3. **Record what works.** A quick note on which build cleared which content saves you from repeating failed experiments.

If you play co-op, remember that group play changes both aggro and drop distribution. Community-driven lootborne guide threads regularly note that the fastest way to lose a random group is to sprint ahead of the stated objective.

## Frequently Asked Questions About This Lootborne Guide

**Is Lootborne beginner-friendly?**

Generally yes, as long as you resist the urge to chase rarity. The early loop teaches you to read affixes and manage inventory, and those two skills carry you through most of the mid-game. This lootborne guide's advice about focused farming applies from your very first session.

**How often should I replace my gear?**

Replace a piece when it either beats your current item on the affixes you care about, or when it unlocks an effect your build is missing. Rarity alone is not a reason to swap.

**Should I play solo or in co-op?**

Solo gives you full control over pacing and loot decisions. Co-op speeds up clears and adds coordination, which is great for farming but harder for testing fragile builds. Many players do both — solo to experiment, co-op to grind.

**What's the biggest mistake new players make?**

Investing upgrade materials too early. Materials are usually the scarcest resource, and spending them on gear you'll replace within a few hours slows your progression more than any bad drop table.
